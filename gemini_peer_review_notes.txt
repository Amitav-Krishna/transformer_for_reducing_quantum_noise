This paper provides an insightful comparison of architectural inductive biases for quantum state denoising, effectively demonstrating that the global attention mechanism of **Transformers** significantly outperforms **MLP** and **CNN** architectures. The use of capacity-matched controls and the Pauli-basis representation experiment provide strong evidence that self-attention is uniquely suited to capturing non-local entanglement.

However, there are significant concerns regarding **computational scalability** and the **optimization difficulties** encountered with hard physical constraints that should be addressed before the paper is accepted.

### Recommendation: Major Revision

The following critiques should be addressed to strengthen the manuscript:

---

### 1. Scalability and the "Exponential Wall"

The element-wise tokenization approach leads to a massive sequence length that scales as  for  qubits.

* 
**Critique:** While the author acknowledges this in Section 7.1, the current results are limited to only 5 qubits. For a 10-qubit system, the model would require 1 trillion operations per layer, which is computationally impossible.


* 
**Revision Requirement:** The author should provide more than a theoretical outlook on mitigations. A small-scale proof-of-concept for one of the proposed mitigations (e.g., **Block-Sparse Attention** or **Hierarchical Tokenization**) on a 6 or 7-qubit system would demonstrate that the architecture is not a "dead end" for practical quantum systems.



### 2. Rigor of the Cholesky-Constraint Comparison

The paper concludes that hard constraints like Cholesky parameterization cause model collapse and optimization failure.

* 
**Critique:** The Transformer Cholesky model used ~153k parameters, which is notably different from the primary 119k parameter post-hoc model. Furthermore, the Cholesky Transformer used **global attention pooling** rather than the successful element-wise projection. This architectural shift may be the cause of the "maximally mixed state" collapse rather than the Cholesky parameterization itself.


* **Revision Requirement:** To definitively claim that post-hoc projection is superior for optimization, the author should test a Cholesky model that maintains the same element-wise output structure as the primary Transformer model, or provide a more detailed analysis of why global pooling was necessary for the Cholesky variant.

### 3. Real-World Noise Applicability

The study utilizes synthetic noise channels (depolarizing, bit-flip, etc.) applied independently in a Markovian fashion.

* **Critique:** Real-world quantum hardware is often plagued by **non-Markovian noise** and **crosstalk**, where errors on one qubit are correlated with others. The current dataset specifically notes that its sequential composition "does not induce non-Markovian dynamics".


* 
**Revision Requirement:** The author should discuss how the global attention mechanism might handle—or potentially struggle with—spatially correlated noise or non-Markovian effects common in NISQ hardware.



### 4. Interpretation of CNN Failure

The paper argues that the CNN's failure (0.68x improvement) is due to its "spatial locality bias" being fundamentally wrong for entanglement.

* 
**Critique:** While the 5-qubit density matrix is only , most modern CNNs can capture global features through sufficient depth or pooling.


* 
**Revision Requirement:** Clarify if the CNN failure was due to a lack of receptive field size or if the "two-channel image" representation truly prevents the kernels from learning the specific off-diagonal correlations of the GHZ state.
