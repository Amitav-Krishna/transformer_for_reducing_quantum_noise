* Introduction

** The Scaling Problem

Prior work (paper v3) established that Transformers outperform MLPs for 5-qubit density matrix denoising (0.28 vs 0.17 Uhlmann fidelity). However, the element-wise tokenization strategy---treating each of 1024 matrix elements as a token---does not scale:

| Qubits | Matrix Size | Tokens | Attention Ops |
|--------|-------------|--------|---------------|
| 5      | 32x32       | 1,024  | ~1M           |
| 6      | 64x64       | 4,096  | ~16M          |
| 8      | 256x256     | 65,536 | ~4B           |

At 8 qubits, element-wise attention is computationally infeasible.

** Our Contribution

We introduce *hierarchical patch-based tokenization* that maintains a fixed token count (64) regardless of system size:
- 5-qubit: 4x4 patches on 32x32 matrix → 64 tokens
- 8-qubit: 32x32 patches on 256x256 matrix → 64 tokens

This enables scaling to larger quantum systems while preserving the attention mechanism's ability to capture non-local correlations.

We further demonstrate through capacity and depth ablations that the Transformer's advantage is architectural, not attributable to parameter count or network depth.

* Methods

** Dataset

*** 5-Qubit Dataset

- Matrix size: 32 × 32 (1024 complex elements)
- 100,000 (noisy, clean) pairs
- 5 noise types × 4 noise levels = 20 cells, 5000 samples/cell
- Noise types: depolarizing, amplitude damping, phase damping, bit-flip, mixed
- Noise levels: p ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20}
- Precision: float64 throughout

*** 8-Qubit Dataset

- Matrix size: 256 × 256 (65,536 complex elements)
- 100,000 (noisy, clean) pairs
- Same noise distribution as 5-qubit
- Storage: ~50GB (streamed during training)

*** Data Split

80/10/10 train/val/test, seed=42 for reproducibility.

** Hierarchical Transformer Architecture

*** Patch Embedding

The input density matrix is divided into non-overlapping patches, each embedded via a linear projection:

- 5-qubit: 4×4 patches → 64 tokens, each patch has 32 complex elements (64 real values)
- 8-qubit: 32×32 patches → 64 tokens, each patch has 2048 complex elements (4096 real values)

Embedding dimension: 128 for both scales.

*** Encoder-Decoder Structure

- Encoder: 4 transformer layers, 8 attention heads, FFN dimension 512
- Decoder: 4 transformer layers, 8 attention heads, cross-attention to encoder
- Total parameters: ~1.09M (5-qubit), ~1.61M (8-qubit)

The parameter difference comes from the patch embedding/unembedding layers, which scale with patch size.

*** Patch Unembedding

Each decoder token is projected back to its corresponding patch via a linear layer, then patches are reassembled into the full matrix.

** Hierarchical MLP Architecture (MLP-Mixer Style)

For fair comparison, we use an MLP with identical patch embedding/unembedding as the Transformer, replacing only the attention mechanism:

- *Token mixing*: MLP that mixes across patches (replaces attention)
- *Channel mixing*: Per-patch MLP (same as Transformer FFN)

This isolates the effect of attention vs. fixed learned mixing weights.

Parameter counts matched to Transformer: ~1.09M (5-qubit), ~1.61M (8-qubit).

** Ablation Models

*** Wide MLP (~5.2M params)

Same architecture as matched MLP but with 4× wider hidden dimensions:
- Tests whether MLP underperformance is due to insufficient capacity

*** Deep MLP (8+8 layers, ~2.15M params)

Same architecture as matched MLP but with 2× depth:
- Tests whether MLP underperformance is due to insufficient depth

** Evaluation Metric

Uhlmann fidelity: $F(\rho, \sigma) = (\mathrm{Tr}[\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}])^2$

Computed on test set after projecting outputs to valid density matrices (Hermitianize → clamp negative eigenvalues → normalize trace).

** 8-Qubit Scaling

#+NAME: tab:8qubit-scaling
#+CAPTION: 8-qubit scaling results reveal unexpected failure mode.
| Model | 5-qubit | 8-qubit | Scaling |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|
| Baseline (noisy) | 0.167 | 0.00839 | --- |
| MLP (hierarchical) | 0.342 | 0.00882 | 1.05× baseline |
| Transformer (hierarchical) | 0.375 | 0.00816 | 0.97× baseline |

*Architecture note*: Both use 64 tokens (4×4 patches for 5-qubit, 32×32 patches for 8-qubit).

*Unexpected result*: Both models fail to learn meaningful denoising on 8-qubit data. The Transformer performs *worse* than the noisy baseline (0.00816 vs 0.00839), while the MLP provides only marginal improvement (1.05×).

*** Analysis of 8-Qubit Failure

Training loss analysis reveals the root cause:

| Model | Initial Val Loss | Final Val Loss | Improvement |
|-------|------------------|----------------|-------------|
| Transformer 8q | 0.935 | 0.904 | 3.3% |
| MLP 8q | 0.938 | 0.902 | 3.8% |
| *5q models (expected)* | ~0.8 | ~0.3 | ~62% |

Both 8-qubit models are essentially stuck---validation loss barely improves over 100 epochs.

Hypothesized causes:
1. *Patch size too large*: 32×32 patches contain 2048 complex values each, overwhelming the embedding capacity
2. *Insufficient parameters per value*: 5-qubit has ~1000 params/value, 8-qubit has only ~25 params/value
3. *Fundamentally different noise structure*: 8-qubit baseline fidelity (0.008) is 20× lower than 5-qubit (0.167), suggesting qualitatively harder denoising problem

* Discussion

** Why Hierarchical Tokenization Works

Patch-based tokenization trades spatial resolution for scalability:
- Loses fine-grained element-level attention within patches
- Preserves global attention across patches (inter-patch correlations)
- Key insight: Most entanglement-relevant correlations span patches, not individual elements

** Capacity vs Architecture

The ablation results (if hypothesis confirmed) demonstrate that:
1. MLP underperformance is not due to insufficient parameters
2. MLP underperformance is not due to insufficient depth
3. The attention mechanism itself captures structure that MLPs cannot

** Limitations

*** Patch Size Tradeoff

Larger patches = fewer tokens = less memory, but coarser attention resolution. The optimal patch size depends on the entanglement structure of the target states.

*** Noise Model Simplicity

Our synthetic noise (Markovian, independent per qubit) may not capture crosstalk and non-Markovian effects in real hardware. However, attention may be well-suited to learn such correlated noise patterns.

*** Maximum System Size

Even with hierarchical tokenization, the patch embedding/unembedding layers scale with patch size. For very large systems (>10 qubits), additional compression may be needed.

2. Model capacity insufficient: only ~25 params/value vs ~1000 for 5-qubit
3. 8-qubit noise fundamentally harder: baseline 20× lower than 5-qubit

*** Implications for Paper

The 8-qubit failure is disappointing but scientifically interesting. Options:
1. Focus paper on 5-qubit results only (clear success story)
2. Include 8-qubit as negative result with analysis of failure modes
3. Design larger 8-qubit model (smaller patches, more params) and retrain
